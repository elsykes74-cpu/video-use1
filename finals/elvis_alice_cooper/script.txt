1970. Las Vegas. Alice Cooper gets a message that stops him cold.

Elvis Presley wants to meet him.

He rides up to the penthouse of the Las Vegas Hilton in an elevator with Liza Minnelli, Chubby Checker, and Linda Lovelace. When the doors open, Elvis's security team frisks every single one of them for weapons.

Which — as Cooper later noted — was completely ridiculous. Because once you got inside the suite, there were guns everywhere.

Elvis walked in. Looked fit. Looked sharp. Zeroed straight in on Cooper.

"Hey man. You're that cat with the snake, ain't ya?"

Then he pulled Cooper aside — away from the group, into the kitchen — opened a drawer, and placed a pistol directly into Cooper's hand.

"Point it at my head," Elvis said. "I'm gonna show you how to take a gun from somebody."

Alice Cooper stood in that kitchen holding a firearm aimed at the King of Rock and Roll.

And in that moment — he later admitted — a voice in his ear said go on. Pull the trigger. You'll always be the guy who killed Elvis.

Another voice said — don't kill him. Just wound him. You'll only get a few years.

Before he could process either thought —

Elvis threw a flying kick. The gun left Cooper's hand. A sweep of the legs. Cooper hit the floor. Elvis's boot pressed against his throat.

Completely calm. Completely in control.

"That's how you stop a man with a gun."

Cooper looked up from the floor and said the only thing he could think of.

"That's good, Elvis."

Elvis Presley just wanted someone to practice his karate on.

Sometimes — Cooper said later — he thinks the guy was just lonely.
